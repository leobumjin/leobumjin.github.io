Someone says that they love books. Yet the only book they have ever read is a single book they deeply cherish. Someone who has read countless books looks at them and says,

> “How can you say you love books when you haven’t even read *The Little Prince*?”

In *The Little Prince*, one rose becomes special among countless others because of the time, care, and affection the Little Prince has devoted to it. What matters is not how many roses one has seen, but which rose one has formed a relationship with and chosen to cherish.

Seen in light of this lesson, the positions of the two readers are strangely reversed. The person who has read countless books, including *The Little Prince*, criticizes another person for loving only one book. Yet in doing so, they fail to apply the very lesson that *The Little Prince* teaches.

The person who has never read *The Little Prince*, meanwhile, has never encountered the lesson of the rose through the book itself. And yet, by cherishing a single book to which they have given their time and affection, they are already living something remarkably close to that lesson.

> **The person who read *The Little Prince* failed to understand the lesson of the rose, while the person who never read it was already living that lesson.**

This is where the paradox emerges. Reading, understanding, and living by a book are not the same thing. A person may have read a book without allowing its ideas to shape the way they see others, while another person may never have encountered those ideas in words and yet live according to something very similar.

Having read a book therefore does not necessarily mean having truly understood it. The distance between reading and understanding becomes most visible when someone knows what a book teaches but behaves in a way that contradicts it.

Knowledge of a lesson can even become another means of judging others, despite the lesson itself asking us to look inward. Those who read books therefore have even more reason to be careful.

> **A book’s lesson should become a mirror before it becomes a ruler.**
